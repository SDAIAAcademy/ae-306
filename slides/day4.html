Deletes and maintenance

A deleted row stays in its file until maintenance removes it

OperationWhat it writesWhat it leaves behind
Delete or update a rowa rewritten data file, or a deletion vector marking the row's positionthe old file, still read by earlier snapshots
Many small appendsmany small data filesslow scans: Day 1's small-files problem
Compactionfewer, larger files with the deletes appliedthe replaced files, until they expire
Expire snapshotsshorter historynothing: files no snapshot needs are deleted

Maintenance is scheduled like any other asset. Until it runs, "deleted" means "hidden from the current snapshot".

Two ways to delete a row. Copy-on-write rewrites the whole data file without the row. Merge-on-read leaves the file and records which rows are gone: position delete files in version 2 of the Iceberg specification, deletion vectors (one bitmap per data file) in version 3; Delta Lake has deletion vectors too. Compaction rewrites small files into larger ones and applies the pending deletes. The stretch of Lab 4.1 expires a snapshot. Keep this slide in mind for Session 4: an erasure request is only finished after the delete, the compaction and the expiry. Source: iceberg.apache.org/spec.

02
Distributed processing

Partitions, shuffles and stages

Partitions

The data split into pieces that workers process in parallel.

Shuffle

Rows move between workers so equal keys meet: for joins and group-bys. The expensive step.

Stages

The work between two shuffles; a stage ends where data must move.

Apache Spark is the production example (Spark 4 at the time of writing; it needs a Java runtime, which is why the lab uses Dask). Ray Data and Daft are others. The vocabulary transfers: partitions, shuffles, stages, broadcast joins.

Each partition of lines meets the whole price list where it is; the month sums combine in a tree.

BlockwiseMerge is Dask's broadcast join. Two large tables would force a hash shuffle: every row crosses to the worker that owns its key. Ask: which Qafila join would shuffle? Lines to shipments, if both were large.

Numbers from the course's full run (peak memory is this process's resident memory above its start). Be fair to Dask: its local cluster here runs threads in one process, so it pays the scheduling overhead without the benefit of more machines. The point is the method: measure on your data before choosing.

The decision

Choose a cluster when one machine cannot hold the work, not before

  • The data, with the working space of the query, no longer fits one machine's memory and disk.
  • A deadline that one machine cannot meet, shown by a measurement.
  • Many independent jobs at once, where the cluster is shared.
Qafila: three months of lines are a few megabytes. Ten years would be under a gigabyte. One node.

The size estimate: 17,665 lines a quarter; even a hundred times Qafila's volume fits a laptop. Ask the room to estimate their own largest table before Lab 4.2, then compare with the measurement.

03
Streaming fundamentals

A log, offsets and consumer groups

  • Producers append events; each gets the next offset.
  • A consumer group commits the offset it will read next, so a restart resumes there.
  • Several groups read the same log independently: operations and finance each have their own position.

Lab 4.3

2,312 shipment events, one partition, read in batches of 100, committed after each batch.

Apache Kafka is the production example; Redpanda and the cloud services speak its protocol. The lab's Q.Log is one partition of such a broker, written as a file so it runs anywhere. Partitions in a real topic give ordering per key, not across the topic.

Lab 4.3 · step 2

A crash before the commit replays the batch: count by key, not by event

747
deliveries, counting events
709
deliveries, by shipment id

The consumer crashed at offset 1,049 with its last commit at 1,000, and the carriers sent 55 events twice. Keyed by shipment, the replay changes nothing.

Exactly-once in the strict sense needs transactions between the log and the sink; most pipelines get the same result with at-least-once delivery and idempotent writes, as Day 2's loads did. Ask which Day 2 lab this repeats: 2.1 and 2.3.

Event time

Event timedelivered on 21 October
→

→

Arrival time would put a late delivery on the wrong day. Engines that implement watermarks: Apache Flink, Spark Structured Streaming, Kafka Streams, and streaming databases. The lab writes the logic in thirty lines so the idea is visible.

Lab 4.3 · step 4 and stretch

A longer watermark answers later and drops less

1 hour
22 dropped
1 day

3 days

Every delivery the stream misses is a late event the watermark dropped; the daily batch recount (709 deliveries) repairs it.

Reconciliation is the habit: the stream gives operations a fast number, the batch gives finance the final one, and the difference is reported, never hidden. A 3-day watermark would emit each day three days late: no faster than the batch.

Why. Operations values speed and accepts a small, known shortfall. Finance's completeness comes from the batch, and the reconciliation keeps the two honest.

Answer: A. The trade-off is the point of the outcome: speed against completeness, chosen per consumer.

04
National Data Classification Policy

Four levels, by the impact of unauthorised disclosure

LevelImpactExamples the policy gives
Top Secrethighnational security, critical infrastructure keys
Secretmediumvital installations, major financial loss
Restricted (older texts: Confidential)lowpersonally identifiable information, bank accounts, supplier contracts and quotations
Publicnonepublic announcements, contact persons at organisations

Issued by the National Data Management Office (part of SDAIA). Restricted is the level that older texts and many internal templates still call Confidential: say so, because participants will meet both words for the same level. It has three sub-levels (A, B, C) by how widely the impact spreads. Principles worth quoting: highest level of protection for a mixed dataset, need to know, least privilege. Sources: sdaia.gov.sa Data Classification Policy; Personal Data Protection Law.

Day 4

Three things to keep from today

Open tables

Snapshots, ids, hidden partitions: two engines, one answer, any point in time.

Measure before you scale

One node until a measurement says otherwise; know your shuffles.

People in the data

Classify every field, minimise, mask per role, test access, erase every copy.

Distributed processing and streaming both served LO4 today: an engine chosen by measurement, and consumers that survive a crash and a late event, are part of a pipeline that fails safely. Idempotent consumers are the same discipline as Day 2's loads.
